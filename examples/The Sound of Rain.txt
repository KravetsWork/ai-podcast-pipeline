The Sound of Rain

Have you ever noticed how the smell of rain on dry ground feels almost nostalgic?
That scent has a name: petrichor. It comes from oils released by plants during dry spells,
combined with a compound called geosmin, produced by soil bacteria.

When the first drops hit the ground, tiny air bubbles trap these molecules and launch them
into the air as a fine aerosol. Our noses are astonishingly sensitive to geosmin: we can detect
it at a few parts per trillion.

So the next time a storm rolls in, take a deep breath. You are smelling chemistry, biology,
and physics working together in a single moment.
